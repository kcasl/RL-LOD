Recently, in the fields of Physical AI, autonomous driving, and robotics, 3D simulation-based learning has become an essential research paradigm due to the high cost of collecting real-world data, safety concerns, and the difficulty of reproducing rare situations. However, rendering all objects with a high Level of Detail (LOD) causes computational bottlenecks and frame rate degradation, while uniformly applying low LOD results in the loss of critical visual information from the agent’s first-person observations, leading to unstable learning and reduced real-world performance. To address the trade-off between rendering efficiency and observation quality, this study proposes a reinforcement learning-based adaptive LOD control framework from a first-person perspective. The proposed method utilizes object distance, on-screen size, visible area ratio, and current FPS as state information, and trains an LOD control policy network using a reward function that considers both visual quality and FPS stability. Experimental results demonstrate that, compared to fixed LOD methods, the proposed approach improves average FPS and frame stability while effectively preserving critical visual information from the agent’s perspective.
